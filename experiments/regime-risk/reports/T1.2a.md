# T1.2a controls

| control | split | result |
|---|---|---|
| cheat, h=4 | train, 25 cells | PASS. Every cell's 95% CI excludes 0. Smallest excess is 13.71 bps (SPY 2013). Largest is 111.64 bps (AMD 2016). |
| random | train, 25 cells | Horizons 2, 4, and 8 include 0 in 25/25 cells. Horizon 78 and 130 include 0 in 24/25. Horizon 26 includes 0 in 22/25 (0.880). |

- Cheat fires on a random 10% of bars whose next 4-bar return is positive, then scores that entry like any other signal.
- Random fires at the stacked density of VSA plus every 55-cell pool hash (about 4,000 to 8,600 fires per cell) and is signed from that direction mix.
- The three h=26 exclusions are SPY 2016 +2.31 bps, QQQ 2010 −3.05 bps, MSFT 2010 −3.35 bps. Two sides, a few bps, on a CI about ±2 bps wide because the stacked sample is huge and random signs cancel the common day.
- The strict line "every horizon ≥ 90% of cells" fails at h=26 by one cell (22/25 rather than 23/25). The point estimates are still about zero.
- Neutral signals were dropped. Round-trip cost in the real-signal table is `2 * $0.01 / close`.

What I am unsure about: a day-block bootstrap of randomly signed 1-day returns is so tight that a 2 bp sample wobble excludes zero. That is a property of stacking every pool hash into one control, not a sign that the matched baseline is shifted.
